Many domains in this list carry one or more **Louw-Nida codes**. They point into the
*Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament Based on Semantic Domains*, edited by Johannes
P. Louw and Eugene A. Nida and published by the United Bible Societies in 1988.

## What the Louw-Nida numbers are

Louw and Nida's lexicon was the first major New Testament dictionary to abandon
alphabetical order. Instead of running from *alpha* to *omega*, it groups every word in the
Greek New Testament by meaning, so that all the words for speaking sit together, all the
kinship terms sit together, and a translator looking for the right word can weigh the whole
set at once.

That vocabulary is divided into 93 numbered top-level domains, from 1 *Geographical Objects
and Features* through to 93 *Names of Persons and Places*. Most are divided again into
lettered subdomains: inside 33 *Communication* you will find 33A *Language*, 33F *Speak,
Talk*, and dozens more.

Because the lexicon covers a single body of first-century text, its coverage is uneven by
design. It is fine-grained where the New Testament is fine-grained — religious activity,
moral qualities, kinship, agriculture, grammatical relations — and silent where the New
Testament is silent. There is no Louw-Nida domain for most of the world's plants and
animals, for modern technology, or for a great deal of ordinary household activity.

## How to read a Louw-Nida code

A code is a number, usually followed by a capital letter, and sometimes by a prime mark.

- **14** is the top-level domain 14, *Physical Events and States*.
- **33F** is subdomain F of domain 33, so *Speak, Talk* within *Communication*.
- **33A&prime;** is a different subdomain from 33A. When a domain runs past 26 subdomains,
  the lexicon continues beyond Z by starting the alphabet again with a prime — A&prime;,
  B&prime;, C&prime; — and then again with a double prime.

The prime marks carry meaning and cannot be dropped. Domain 33 *Communication* is large
enough to use all three sequences, so 33A *Language*, 33A&prime; *Advise* and
33A&Prime; *Prophesy* are three unrelated subdomains, not three spellings of one.

## Why this list refers to Louw-Nida

Louw and Nida's work is a direct ancestor of the list published here. Les Bruce and his
team built the semantic-domain list used in LinguaLinks — now
[FieldWorks](http://fieldworks.sil.org/) — on top of Louw and Nida's domains, adding about
a hundred of their own; the list on this site grew out of that work in turn.

Recording the Louw-Nida codes alongside each domain lets a user check this list against one
of its sources, and lets a Bible translator move in both directions: from a domain here to
the corresponding set of Greek words, and back again.

## The mapping is approximate

> A Louw-Nida code on a domain page means *these two domains cover roughly the same area of
> meaning*. It is a signpost for further reading, not a claim that the two are equivalent.

The relationship is not one-to-one in either direction. One domain here may carry several
Louw-Nida codes, because this list draws distinctions the lexicon has no need for. One
Louw-Nida code may appear on several domains here, for the same reason in reverse. And most
domains carry no code at all, usually because the New Testament does not discuss the
subject.

Where the two disagree, the description and elicitation questions on the domain page are
what govern this list.
